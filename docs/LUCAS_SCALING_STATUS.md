# Lucas scaling status — independent verification ledger

## Defensible form of the claim
Lucas and Pisot share one exponent, \(n\cdot\log_2\phi\). That exponent is the shared scaling unit of every quantity generated by the golden recurrence: arithmetic ceiling, representation precision, information capacity, and the spectral gap. It is not a claim that Lucas reduces the iteration count of a fixed search problem.

## Confirmed by independent computation
- \(|\phi^n - L_n| = |\psi|^n\) exactly (to float64 limit).
- \(\log_2(L_n) = n\cdot\log_2\phi\) (ratio variance \(3.1\times10^{-11}\)).
- \(L_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n+1}\) at every tested rung.
- \(L_n / F_{n+1} \to 1.381966\) (constant fractional position between consecutive Fibonacci numbers). \(L_n\) is not itself a Fibonacci number.
- Information capacity \(\log_2 L_n \to n\cdot\log_2\phi\).
- Spectral gap \(1 - \phi/2 = 1/(2\phi^2)\), verified to \(10^{-12}\).
- Coverage parity: odd \(L_n\) partition a power-of-two estate exactly; even \(L_n\) (n=20, n=50) yield \(\gcd=2\) and cover only half.

## Refuted or not supported
- Decorative phase multiplier at fixed \(N\): no gain.
- Lane indexing over a fixed estate: costs work in the scheme tested.
- \(L_n\) is atomic (single Zeckendorf term / itself Fibonacci): false. It is a boundary marker.
- Structure-control \(\chi^2\) test: badly posed, discarded.

## Never yet demonstrated
That any of the above reduces the iteration count of a fixed search problem.
