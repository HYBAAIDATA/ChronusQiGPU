# Acceleration review

Scope: [REVIEW FIRST](https://app.notion.com/p/413afc1782e880ef8137efd45a7cd026), the mathematical exposition supplied with the work request, and the existing [claim audit](../docs/claim-audit.md). REVIEW FIRST sets the evidence and delivery standard; it is not a detailed mathematical specification of the acceleration layer. The mathematical exposition is part of the work request, not the text of that page. This is an analytical review, not evidence of a working implementation, measured acceleration, a security assessment, or a release approval.

## What the page captures correctly

- Extraordinary comparative, security and customer claims require attributable, reproducible evidence. Separating design from demonstrated capability and tying claims to code versions, test results and explicit refusals is sound.
- Seven complementary test classes, a coverage gate, an auditable chain of evidence and customer-verifiable results are valuable delivery disciplines. Coverage is a useful diagnostic, not a measure of correctness. Customer-specific results must be measured on an agreed workload.
- The supplied exposition correctly states normalized state vectors, unitary norm preservation in exact arithmetic, the basic Fourier transform, singular-value and Schmidt decompositions, and the variational upper-bound principle, subject to their usual assumptions. Structured low-rank tensor representations can be economical on workloads with bounded bond dimension and exploitable structure. Mathematical descriptions are legitimate independent objects of study.
- The existence of shared mathematical structures can motivate reusable interfaces across domains, but each domain's mapping and outcomes need their own validation.

## What the page overstates

- Neither the page's ontology nor the supplied exposition demonstrates that one mathematical foundation solves storage, security and health problems, that the golden ratio is necessary, or that advantage follows from mathematical description alone. Each needs a precise problem, input/output model, algorithm, assumptions, proof and reproducible comparator.
- A vector of dimension N has N amplitudes; materializing arbitrary amplitudes requires resources proportional to N, and reading an N-entry answer has an output-size lower bound. A compact circuit representation or a large state space does not by itself give an efficiently accessible exponential output. Applying a dense N by N matrix is not a free operation.
- The Fourier transform of N explicit inputs with N explicit outputs admits an O(N log N) FFT; comparing that end-to-end task to a gate count alone ignores input preparation and output extraction. Special compact-input or sample-output problems must be stated separately. Phase estimation's precision requires controlled powers of U; counting only the number of distinct power operations hides the potentially O(1/epsilon) cost of implementing them at precision epsilon.
- Amplitude amplification gives a quadratic *oracle-query* bound for unstructured search under a specified oracle model, not an unconditional measured runtime advantage. Evaluating and applying the oracle and obtaining the answer must be counted. With an explicit unsorted list and no further structure, linear inspection is optimal in the worst case; an index can change the problem. Quantum-walk performance depends on the graph, observable and access model; no universal mixing-time formula applies.
- Bounded-rank tensor contraction or low-rank approximation can be efficient for particular structured inputs and specified errors; generic states can require exponentially growing rank. SVD gives optimal low-rank approximation for a fixed matrix and norm, not universal exponential compression. For explicit inputs, mature FFT, SVD and indexed-search methods are strong comparators, not automatic losers.
- Variational upper bounds do not imply polynomial convergence or global optimality; QAOA, VQE and geometric optimization have no general exponential speedup guarantee. Natural gradients require a specified metric and stable treatment of singularity and conditioning. The supplied pure-state metric omits the imaginary components in its second term; the gauge-invariant expression is 4 Re(<∂iψ|∂jψ> − <∂iψ|ψ><ψ|∂jψ>). Geodesic distance alone does not establish computational cost.
- Category-theoretic composition and topological invariants provide useful formalisms, not automatic synthesis speedups, security or fault tolerance. The displayed homology sequence is not generally short exact as written. Error correction requires a specified code, noise model, decoding procedure and demonstrated bounds. Feature dimension or kernel accessibility alone does not establish predictive or computational advantage.
- Randomized tests and finite bounded exhaustive tests demonstrate only the tested cases, not universal theorems. A signature, hash or verification endpoint proves neither claim accuracy nor independent accreditation. Seven test labels, an executive pack, and coverage cannot establish a customer reference, authorization, or market leadership.

## What the page understates

- Measurements must identify task equivalence, full input/output costs, comparator, dataset provenance, repeated raw timings, uncertainty, warm-up, numerical tolerance, environment, and limitations; retain correctness checks alongside timings. A speedup is a measured ratio for that workload, never a universal claim.
- Approximation must expose rank, discarded singular values, an explicit norm and an error bound; normalization, floating-point drift, dimension limits and invalid inputs need observable failure behavior. A security posture needs a threat model, dependency and vulnerability assessment, resource limits and independent review.
- Evidence integrity requires a reproducible hash manifest and a real, independently verifiable, standards-conformant signing implementation with trusted key custody. The existing claim audit explicitly withdraws the repository's ML-DSA conformance claim; a verification URL or simulated signer must not be presented as satisfying that requirement.
- Customer reports should distinguish synthetic tests from customer-authorized workloads, document refusal conditions and disclose unverified controls, missing independent assessments and operational limits. Accreditation, a signed SLA, a real reference customer and procurement acceptance require accountable parties and cannot be manufactured by a test run.

## What the page leaves open

- **Problem classes:** Which customer inputs, outputs, accuracy and performance targets? Which bounded-rank, sparse, Fourier, search, geometric, topological, learning and variational cases are in scope? Which are expressly refused? What baseline and workload distribution decide advantage?
- **Deployment model:** What service boundary, tenancy, identity, data residency, operational ownership, rollback, retention, resource limits and threat model apply? Which existing interfaces are safe to integrate?
- **Pricing model:** What is the unit of value, metering policy, cost basis, service commitment and commercial approval? None is established by the page.
- **Reference customer:** Who has consented to a workload, a reproducible evaluation and public reference? No customer or result is established here.
- **Acceptance and evidence:** Which independent assessor, control owners, control framework scope, signing keys and authorization authority will provide the missing security and government evidence? Who accepts the numerical-error bounds, operational risks and benchmark protocol?

Recommendation: implement only precisely bounded, testable mathematical operations with explicit cost and refusal surfaces, measure against equivalent baseline tasks, and mark all unverified commercial, security and authorization requirements as blockers. Do not equate successful local tests with production qualification.
