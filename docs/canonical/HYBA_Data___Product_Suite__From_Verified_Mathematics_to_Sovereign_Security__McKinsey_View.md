## 1. Why now — the attack surface argument

The NHS breach (and the broader pattern of ransomware against critical national infrastructure) succeeded against systems that were compliant, patched, and perimeter-defended. That's the tell: the paradigm itself — static encryption, signature-based detection, human-paced incident response — is the vulnerability, not any single control. Three structural shifts define the opportunity:

1. **Encryption is a time-bomb, not a wall.** Classical public-key crypto is a harvest-now-decrypt-later target; "quantum-safe" vendors are mostly bolting on bigger keys, not rethinking the architecture.
2. **Detection is reactive.** Signature/anomaly systems find breaches after the fact; they don't change the cost structure of attacking in the first place.
3. **Remediation is human-paced.** Incident response measured in hours-to-days is structurally too slow against automated, AI-assisted attackers.

HYBA's premise: mathematics that is **substrate-agnostic** (quantum structure implemented on classical hardware, not dependent on quantum computers existing) can attack all three simultaneously — topological rather than encryption-only data protection, math-native anomaly detection, and self-healing systems that remediate before a human is paged.
