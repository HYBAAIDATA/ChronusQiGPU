## 1. The Core Dynamic

The value of the PHI-Space substrate changes fundamentally depending on **where** in the stack it is deployed. 

We have two primary user archetypes:

1. **The Enterprise (e.g., JP Morgan):** Deploys HYBA at the *Application/SDK layer* to reduce their cloud spend.
2. **The Hyperscaler (e.g., AWS, Azure, GCP):** Deploys HYBA at the *Hypervisor/Infrastructure layer* to expand their sellable capacity.

They are both using the exact same $\phi^4$ ($6.85\times$) compression, but the economic outcome is inverted.

---

## 2. Archetype A: The Enterprise (JP Morgan)

**The Goal:** Cost Reduction & Data Sovereignty

When JP Morgan integrates the HYBA Data SDK into their internal applications, the data is folded *before* it leaves their servers. 

- **The Mechanism:** JPM holds the fractal boundary. They send a 100 TB dataset to AWS. Because the SDK compressed it by $6.85\times$, AWS only receives 14.6 TB of folded $\phi$-states.
- **The Result:** JPM's AWS storage and compute bill drops by 85%. AWS loses revenue because JPM is consuming less physical space.
- **HYBA's Revenue:** We invoice JPM for 25% of the AWS bill they just avoided.

## 3. Archetype B: The Hyperscaler (AWS)

**The Goal:** Fleet Capacity Expansion & Margin Multiplier

Hyperscalers are currently engaged in a massive, capital-intensive war to build more data centers to handle the AI boom. They are running out of land, power, and chips. 

When AWS licenses HYBA Data, they do not give it to their customers. They install PULVINI and HERMES directly into their hypervisor (e.g., their Elastic Block Store or EC2 backend).

- **The Mechanism:** The integration is completely transparent to the end-user. An AWS customer uploads 100 PB of raw data and pays AWS for 100 PB of storage. But under the hood, AWS applies the Golden Ratio Transform, folding that 100 PB down to 14.6 PB on their physical drives.
- **The Result:** AWS can now sell **685 PB** of logical storage capacity to their customers, while only paying the Capital Expenditure (servers, electricity, real estate) to house **100 PB** of physical hardware.
- **HYBA's Revenue:** AWS has artificially expanded their fleet capacity by $6.85\times$ without building a single new data center. HYBA takes a 25% gain-share on the net-new revenue generated by this phantom capacity.

## 5. Archetype C: B2C Single Instance (MacCore Edge Node)

**The Goal:** Consumer Hardware Expansion

Individuals download the HYBA MacCore daemon to turn their personal MacBooks, Windows laptops, or mobile devices into supercomputers.

- **The Mechanism:** The OS-level daemon intercepts memory allocation, expanding a 16GB laptop into 109.6GB of effective memory via $6.85\times$ compression.
- **The Revenue:** Billed via a fixed consumer subscription (e.g., $9.99/mo) rather than a dynamic gain-share, providing highly predictable, massive-scale recurring revenue across millions of edge devices.

## 6. Archetype D: Enterprise Fleet (B2B2C Managed Devices)

**The Goal:** Corporate Hardware Life Extension

An enterprise subscribes not just for their cloud infrastructure, but for their employees' endpoint devices.

- **The Mechanism:** A company with 10,000 employees deploys the HYBA edge client via MDM (Mobile Device Management) to all corporate laptops. Instead of upgrading the fleet's hardware, every employee instantly receives $6.85\times$ more RAM and local AI processing capability.
- **The Revenue:** A fleet-discounted per-device subscription, scaled across thousands of seats.
