\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Thesis: quantum is the mathematics.} The quantum mechanics that quantum algorithms use is a mathematical
structure: state vectors, unitary evolution, phase inversion, interference, and reduction by symmetry. It is not a
cryostat or a particular piece of silicon. This is one instance of the authors' architectural law, stated in their
\code{unified\_quantum\_engine.py} (Section~\ref{sec:law}). Its first part is that quantum mechanics is the operator
algebra. Its second part is ``no royal road to geometry'': every problem class passes through one three-layer
channel, and a new domain is declarative data, never new core algebra. The engine's own tests verify the second part
for the domains they exhibit; universality is a design principle. We make the first part concrete, and exact, for
Grover amplitude amplification.

When the state space carries the symmetry of a single marked class ($M$ of $N$ states), the unitary dynamics restrict
exactly to a two-dimensional invariant subspace~\cite{grover,bbht,nc}. With amplitudes scaled by $\sqrt N$, those
dynamics are a rational recurrence on $(\alpha_t,\beta_t)\in\Q^2$. We execute that recurrence exactly on an ordinary
CPU: phase inversion, then inversion about the mean, with norm conservation asserted at every step. There is no
floating point, no $\pi$ and no truncation, and for $N<64$ the full $2^N$-amplitude vector is stepped alongside and
agrees at every step.

The peak is found by exact stepping and certified by an independent $\pi$-free Chebyshev criterion: $N=16\to3$
iterations with $P=63001/65536$, $N=4096\to50$, $N=65536\to201$. \textbf{``$O(1)$'' means precisely $O(1)$ amplitude
state per step, made possible by the symmetry.} It is memory per step. The iteration count is still
$\Theta(\sqrt{N/M})$, and the rationals' bit length grows linearly in $t\log N$, so it does not mean $O(1)$ time or
$O(1)$ bit operations.

\textbf{The thesis is only as strong as its separation, so we measure both sides.} The amplitude algebra runs exactly
on any substrate. The oracle is the problem's interface, and its cost is set by the oracle's structure, not by the
algebra. On classical hardware the marked count must be learned by evaluating the oracle on all $W$ candidates, so
oracle cost is $\Theta(W)$. Exact amplitude algebra therefore gives \textbf{no query speed-up} on classical hardware;
that needs a coherent quantum oracle.

The production pipeline has four parts:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Hensel-lifted matrix $M_k\in\Gamma(2)\le\GL(2,\Z/2^k\Z)$, of order $2^{k-1}$, with inverse $M_k-\tr(M_k)I$
because $\det M_k=-1$;
\item a factored $O(N)$ register that folds, and never truncates, $2^N$ states;
\item a bijective Lucas--Pisano schedule;
\item SHA-256 hash-chained THESEUS receipts.
\end{itemize}
On 500 Bitcoin mainnet headers at $k=32$, it finds and re-verifies every winner at $W=4096$ and $W=65536$, using $W$
SHA-256d evaluations per window. That is about twice a linear scan's expected cost.

Beyond mining, $M_k\equiv I+2Q\pmod 4$, where $Q$ is the Fibonacci matrix. This makes orbit, trace, order-finding and
polynomial oracles 2-adically continuous, so generator collapse performs Hensel lifting and cuts oracle evaluations
from $W$ to a few dozen. That saving is classical: Hensel lifting and Pohlig--Hellman match or beat it. SHA-256d and
dense subset-sum oracles lack the structure.

\textbf{The mathematics is golden by construction.} Each golden identity does a specific job:
\begin{itemize}
\item Binet gives $M_{15}=Q^{15}\bmod256$;
\item Cassini gives $\det=-1$ and so the inverse $M-\tr\cdot I$, which is OVID's unfold map $((135,98),(98,37))$;
\item Lucas gives $\tr M_{15}=L_{15}\equiv84$ and the odd schedule strides;
\item Pisano gives the period $3\cdot2^{D-1}$ and $\operatorname{ord}M_8=384/\gcd(15,384)=128$;
\item Pisot makes $L_n$ the exact integer anchor of $\varphi^n$.
\end{itemize}
The Grover iterate and the Fibonacci matrix obey the same Dickson trace recurrence. The Grover iterate is elliptic
($\det 1$, a rotation), while $Q$ is hyperbolic ($\det -1$, the Pisot regime). That is an exact identity, not a
speed-up.

We sweep $S=10^7,\dots,10^{76}$. The production register runs at every ring $k=\lceil\log_2S\rceil$ up to 253, with
exact custody. The Grover peak for $N=S$ is known three ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item by exact stepping, at $10^7$;
\item by an exact integer Chebyshev certificate, at $10^7$, $10^{10}$ and $10^{12}$;
\item by a $\pi$-free rational-interval angle certificate, at all nine scales, up to
$t^*=78539816339744830961566084581987572104$ at $10^{76}$.
\end{itemize}
A quantum query advantage requires quantum hardware.
\end{abstract}

\section{Thesis: Quantum Is the Mathematics}\label{sec:thesis}
\subsection{Statement}
A quantum algorithm is specified by algebra: a state space, unitary operators, and the Born rule. Grover's
algorithm~\cite{grover} on $N$ states uses two reflections:
\begin{itemize}
\item the phase inversion $O=I-2\Pi_{\mathrm{marked}}$, which flips the sign of the $M$ marked amplitudes;
\item the diffusion $D=2|s\rangle\langle s|-I$, which inverts every amplitude about the mean. This is where
interference happens.
\end{itemize}
Both are real orthogonal matrices. Let $|\mathrm{good}\rangle$ and $|\mathrm{bad}\rangle$ be the normalised uniform
superpositions over the marked and the unmarked states. The two-dimensional subspace
$\mathrm{span}\{|\mathrm{good}\rangle,|\mathrm{bad}\rangle\}$ is invariant under $O$ and $D$, and the start state
lies in it~\cite{bbht,nc}. In the scaled coordinates $(\alpha,\beta)$, where the amplitude is $\alpha/\sqrt N$ on
each marked state and $\beta/\sqrt N$ on each unmarked one, the iterate $G=DO$ is the rational map of
Section~\ref{sec:grover}:
\[ \mu=(U\beta-M\alpha)/N,\qquad \alpha'=2\mu+\alpha,\qquad \beta'=2\mu-\beta. \]
The dynamics are therefore defined over $\Q$. Any machine that performs exact rational arithmetic executes them
exactly: a CPU, an integrated GPU, or a quantum device. That is the sense in which we say the mathematics is the
quantum part, and it is substrate- and hardware-agnostic. Our implementation (\code{triad\_grover.py:36}) asserts the
unitarity invariant $M\alpha^2+U\beta^2=N$ exactly at every step (:59). For $N<64$ it steps the full
$2^N$-dimensional rational vector alongside and checks equality, so the fold and the unfolded dynamics agree exactly,
not approximately.

\subsection{The rigorous separation, and why it strengthens the thesis}\label{sec:sep}
A thesis about mathematics should be stated with mathematical precision about what it does and does not imply.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{What is $O(1)$.} The state per step is two rationals, and that is a consequence of symmetry. A generic
quantum state without such symmetry needs up to $2^N$ amplitudes. The fold is a symmetry reduction, not a general
compression of quantum computation. \textbf{$O(1)$ is memory per step; the number of steps is
$\Theta(\sqrt{N/M})$}, the same as Grover's query count, so the classical run time is never below $\Theta(\sqrt N)$
steps (Section~\ref{sec:scales}).
\item \textbf{Bit growth.} The rationals' denominators grow: 17, 1013 and 5645 bits at the peaks for $N=16$, 4096
and 65536 (Section~\ref{sec:grover}). The cost per step in bit operations grows linearly in $t\log N$.
\item \textbf{The oracle is not the algebra.} The marked set enters only through $\Pi_{\mathrm{marked}}$. To
instantiate the fold, $M$ must be known. On classical hardware that costs $W$ oracle evaluations, $\Theta(W)$ for an
unstructured oracle such as SHA-256d (Section~\ref{sec:mining}). A quantum device queries the oracle coherently,
about $(\pi/4)\sqrt{N/M}$ times. That query advantage is a property of a physical, coherent oracle, and it is not
reproduced, or claimed, on classical hardware.
\item \textbf{Prior work.} The two-dimensional reduction is classical knowledge~\cite{grover,bbht,nc}. Our
contribution is the exact, $\pi$-free and attested realisation inside a production pipeline, and the measured
boundary below.
\item \textbf{Structure is classical.} Where an oracle has 2-adic structure (Corollary~\ref{cor:cont}), generator
collapse prunes exponentially. Classical algorithms (Hensel lifting, Pohlig--Hellman) achieve the same pruning, so it
is not evidence that quantum hardware would do better (Sections~\ref{sec:stress} and~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{enumerate}
Separating the substrate-agnostic amplitude algebra from the substrate- and structure-dependent oracle cost is what
makes the thesis checkable. Every claim on the algebra side is an exact identity that we test. Every claim on the
oracle side is an exact count that we measure.

\section{The Hensel-lifted matrix $M_k$}\label{sec:mk}
\begin{definition}
\code{hensellift\_m15(k)} (\code{src/chronus/kernels/gl2\_transform.py:60}) returns
$M_k=\begin{pmatrix}219&98\\98&c_k\end{pmatrix}$ with $c_k=(98^2-1)\cdot219^{-1}\bmod 2^k$, for every $k\ge8$. \code{FoldedRegisterN} accepts $8\le k\le255$ (Section~\ref{sec:scales}).
Then $c_8=121$, and $M_8=M_{15}=Q^{15}\bmod 256$, where $Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and
$Q^n=\begin{pmatrix}F_{n+1}&F_n\\F_n&F_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}$. The values $F_{16}=987$, $F_{15}=610$, $F_{14}=377$ reduce
to $219,98,121$. The construction gives $\det M_k\equiv-1\pmod{2^k}$, $M_k\equiv M_{15}\pmod{256}$, and
$\tr M_8=340\equiv84=L_{15}\pmod{256}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Level-2 congruence subgroup]\label{prop:gamma2}
$M_k\equiv I\pmod 2$. Moreover $M_k=I+2A$ with $A\equiv Q\pmod 2$, i.e.\ $M_k\equiv I+2Q\pmod 4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$(219-1)/2=109$ and $98/2=49$ are odd. The value $c_k$ is odd because $\det\equiv-1$ forces $219c_k$ odd, and
$(c_k-1)/2$ is even because $M_k\equiv M_{15}\pmod{256}$ forces $c_k\equiv121\pmod{256}$, hence
$c_k\equiv1\pmod4$, for every $k\ge8$. So $A\bmod 2=Q$.
\end{proof}
So $M_k\in\Gamma(2):=\ker\bigl(\GL(2,\Z/2^k)\to\GL(2,\Z/2)\bigr)$. Note that $\Gamma(2)$ is taken in $\GL_2$,
because $\det M_k=-1$.

\begin{lemma}[Squaring]\label{lem:sq}
If $X=I+2^jB$ with $j\ge2$ and $B\equiv I\pmod 2$, then $X^2=I+2^{j+1}B'$ with $B'\equiv I\pmod 2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$X^2=I+2^{j+1}(B+2^{j-1}B^2)$, and $2^{j-1}B^2\equiv0\pmod 2$ for $j\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Order $2^{k-1}$]\label{thm:order}
For $k\ge3$, $M_k$ has multiplicative order exactly $2^{k-1}$. More precisely, $M_k^{2^t}=I+2^{t+1}B_t$ with
$B_t\equiv I\pmod2$ for $t\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$M^2=I+4(A+A^2)$, and $A+A^2\equiv Q+Q^2=2Q+I\equiv I\pmod 2$, using $Q^2=Q+I$. Lemma~\ref{lem:sq} then gives the
form of $M^{2^t}$ for every $t\ge1$. For $t=k-2$ we get $M^{2^{k-2}}=I+2^{k-1}B$ with $B$ having odd diagonal, so it
is not $I$ mod $2^k$. For $t=k-1$ we get $M^{2^{k-1}}\equiv I$. Since $\Gamma(2)$ is a 2-group, the order is a power
of 2, hence exactly $2^{k-1}$. (Checked for $k=8,16,32,64$ and at the nine scale rings $k=24,\dots,253$; for $k=8$ the order is 128.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[2-adic continuity]\label{cor:cont}
$M_k^{x+m2^t}\equiv M_k^x\pmod{2^{t+1}}$ for all $x,m,t\ge0$. Hence $x\mapsto M_k^x v$ is continuous with shift~1: its
residue mod $2^{t+1}$ depends only on $x\bmod2^t$. If $v\equiv0\pmod{2^s}$, the shift is $1+s$. This is the
structural property exploited in Section~\ref{sec:stress}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Cayley--Hamilton inverse]\label{prop:ch}
Every $M\in\GL(2,\Z/2^k)$ satisfies $M^2-\tr(M)M+\det(M)I=0$, so $M^{-1}=\det(M)^{-1}\bigl(\tr(M)I-M\bigr)$.
With $\det M=-1$ this becomes
\[ M^{-1}=M-\tr(M)\,I. \]
\end{proposition}
The code implements exactly this formula. \code{invert\_gl2\_mod2k} (\code{gl2\_transform.py:74}) tests
\code{det == mask} (i.e.\ $\det\equiv-1$) and returns $((m_{00}-\tr,m_{01}),(m_{10},m_{11}-\tr))$ at lines 88--93.
That matrix equals $\begin{pmatrix}-c&b\\b&-a\end{pmatrix}=-\operatorname{adj}M$, consistent with $\det^{-1}=-1$.
Other units use $\det^{-1}\operatorname{adj}M$ (lines 94--99), and the result is verified by an explicit product
$MM^{-1}=I$ (lines 101--106). The sign is therefore handled correctly: writing $M^{-1}=\tr(M)I-M$ would be wrong by a
factor $-1$ when $\det=-1$.

\section{\texttt{FoldedRegisterN}: an $O(N)$ factored register (``we fold, we never truncate'')}
\code{FoldedRegisterN} (\code{gl2\_transform.py:740}) represents the $2^N$ states
\[ s(i)=b+\textstyle\sum_j \mathrm{bit}_j(i)\,g_j\in(\Z/2^k)^2,\qquad i\in[0,2^N), \]
by the $N+1$ vectors $(b,g_0,\dots,g_{N-1})$, plus a resolved mask and resolved bits.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Folding} (\code{fold\_mk}, :814) applies $M_k$ to the $N+1$ vectors. By linearity this folds all $2^N$
states, at a cost of $N+1$ matrix--vector products.
\item \textbf{Unfolding} (:817) applies the Cayley--Hamilton inverse.
\item \textbf{Evaluation} (\code{evaluate\_index}, :821) costs $O(N)$ additions per index.
\item \textbf{Collapse} (\code{collapse\_generator}, :842) absorbs $g_j$ into the base when the bit is 1, zeroes $g_j$
and records the bit. Collapse commutes with folding.
\item \textbf{Serialisation} (\code{to\_wire\_bytes}, :873) is lossless: an 18-byte header plus $2\lceil k/8\rceil(N+1)$ bytes,
inside a \code{GL2\textbackslash x01} v2 frame with CRC-32C. For $k=32$ and $N=12$ the frame is 142 bytes.
\end{itemize}
There is no truncating code path. The lossy lane-0 projection exists only in a test-only audit fixture.

\section{The Lucas--Pisano geodesic schedule}\label{sec:sched}
The visit order at epoch $e$ (\code{src/chronus/geodesic/scheduler.py:80,100}) is built as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\pi=\pi(2^D)$ and $\mathit{phase}=e\bmod\pi$;
\item $\mathit{idx}=3\,\mathit{phase}+1$;
\item $\mathit{stride}=L_{\mathit{idx}}\bmod2^D$ and $\mathit{off}=F_{\mathit{idx}}\bmod2^D$;
\item $\mathit{order}[t]=\mathrm{gray}((\mathit{stride}\cdot t+\mathit{off})\bmod2^D)$, where $\mathrm{gray}(x)=x\oplus(x\gg1)$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}
$L_n$ is even if and only if $3\mid n$. Hence $\gcd(L_{3m+1},2^D)=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$L_n\bmod 2$ runs $0,1,1,0,1,1,\dots$ ($L_0=2$, $L_1=1$), with period 3.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pisano period~\cite{wall}]\label{thm:pisano}
$\pi(2)=3$ and $\pi(2^D)=3\cdot2^{D-1}$ for $D\ge2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\pi(m)$ is the order of $Q$ mod $m$. $Q^3=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}=I+2Q$, so
$Q^6=I+4(Q+Q^2)=I+4(I+2Q)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:sq} with $B=I+2Q\equiv I$ gives $Q^{3\cdot2^{D-1}}=I+2^DB'\equiv I$ and
$Q^{3\cdot2^{D-2}}\not\equiv I\pmod{2^D}$. For $D=2$, $Q^3=I+2Q\not\equiv I\pmod4$. The order mod 2 is 3, so the
order mod $2^D$ is $3\cdot2^j$ for some $j$, and the two facts force $j=D-1$. (Checked for $D=1,\dots,20$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Bijection]
An affine map $t\mapsto\mathit{stride}\cdot t+\mathit{off}$ with odd stride is a bijection of $\Z/2^D$, and gray is a
bijection of $[0,2^D)$. The schedule is their composition, so it visits every state exactly once.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{The Hamming spread, stated truthfully.}
Gray coding gives single-bit steps only between consecutive integers. The measured step histograms are exact for
$D=12$ and $D=16$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Epoch 0} (stride $L_1=1$): $\{1:2^D-1\}$, a pure Gray walk. This is the epoch the pipeline uses,
because staged handles have version~0.
\item \textbf{Epoch 1} (stride $L_4=7$): $\{1:2^{D-2}-1,\ 3:3\cdot2^{D-2}\}$.
\item \textbf{Epoch 2} (stride $L_7=29$): $\{1:4096,\ 3:36863,\ 5:24576\}$ at $D=16$.
\end{itemize}
The schedule is a permutation, so it cannot change the expected position of a uniformly placed winner.

\section{Exact two-amplitude Grover}\label{sec:grover}
Write the state as $N^{-1/2}(\alpha$ on each of the $M$ marked states, $\beta$ on each of the $U=N-M$ unmarked
states$)$, starting from $(\alpha_0,\beta_0)=(1,1)$. One Grover iterate is an oracle sign flip followed by inversion
about the mean (\code{src/chronus/quantum/triad\_grover.py:36}):
\[ \mu=\frac{U\beta-M\alpha}{N},\qquad \alpha'=2\mu+\alpha,\qquad \beta'=2\mu-\beta. \]
\textbf{Invariant.} $M\alpha^2+U\beta^2=N$; it is asserted exactly at every step (:59). $P_t=M\alpha_t^2/N$.

\begin{theorem}[Closed form, $\pi$-free]\label{thm:cheb}
Let $c=1-2M/N\in\Q$, which is $\cos2\theta$ for $\sin^2\theta=M/N$, and let $T_n$ be the Chebyshev polynomials. Then
$P_t=\bigl(1-T_{2t+1}(c)\bigr)/2$, and the recurrence agrees with this at every step. In particular
$P_3=63001/65536$ for $N=16$, $M=1$ (with $\alpha_3=251/64$, $\beta_3=-13/64$), which equals
$(7s-56s^3+112s^5-64s^7)^2$ for $s=1/4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The iterate acts on $\mathrm{span}\{|\mathrm{good}\rangle,|\mathrm{bad}\rangle\}$ as a rotation by
$2\theta$~\cite{bbht,nc}, so $P_t=\sin^2((2t+1)\theta)=(1-\cos((4t+2)\theta))/2$, and $\cos(n\varphi)=T_n(\cos\varphi)$
with $\varphi=2\theta$. The identity is verified in exact rationals for every step of every trajectory in the test
suite.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact peak by stepping]\label{thm:peak}
For $M\le N/2$, \code{exact\_grover\_two\_amplitude\_trajectory} (:46) stops at the first $t$ with
$P_{t+1}\le P_t$. That $t$ equals $t^*=\min\{t\ge0:\ T_{t+1}(c)\le0\}$, which is the iteration where
$(2t+2)\theta$ first reaches $\pi/2$. It is the maximiser of $P$ over the first rising run.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
With $\varphi_t=(2t+1)\theta$, we have $P_{t+1}\le P_t\iff|\varphi_{t+1}-\pi/2|\ge|\varphi_t-\pi/2|\iff
\varphi_t+\varphi_{t+1}\ge\pi\iff\cos((2t+2)\theta)=T_{t+1}(c)\le0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Boundary and limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Classical oracle cost.} The two-amplitude recurrence needs $M$. Classically, $M$ costs $W$ oracle
evaluations, so on unstructured oracles such as SHA-256d the pipeline is $\Theta(W)$, about $2\times$ a linear scan's
expected first-hit cost (Section~\ref{sec:mining}). The exact recurrence removes floating-point drift and $\pi$; it
does not remove oracle work.
\item \textbf{Structure pruning is classical.} Where Corollary~\ref{cor:cont} makes the oracle 2-adically continuous,
generator collapse is Hensel lifting~\cite{koblitz}, and it cuts $W$ evaluations to tens. The same saving, often a
larger one, comes from classical algorithms with no amplitude stage: Pohlig--Hellman in the 2-group
$\langle M_k\rangle$~\cite{ph}, Newton--Hensel, back-substitution, and repeated squaring. The quantum part of the
architecture contributes nothing to these wins.
\item \textbf{Ideal Grover versus classical.} Black-box Grover ignores structure, so (q) is identical for structured
and unstructured instances. Classical structured methods beat (q) except in three places:
\begin{itemize}
\item trace fibres, where our classical figure is an upper bound and a polynomial-in-$k$ route exists
[citation needed];
\item $x^2=r$ at $W=4096$ only;
\item dense subset sum. Here quantum walks are known to improve the exponent over the best classical algorithms:
$2^{0.241n}$~\cite{bjlm} and $2^{0.226n}$~\cite{hm}, against $2^{0.291n}$~\cite{bcj}.
\end{itemize}
That advantage requires a fault-tolerant quantum computer and was not exercised here.
\item \textbf{Any gain from quantum search needs quantum hardware.} Nothing in this work runs on, or simulates beyond
bookkeeping, a quantum device. The two-amplitude state is the exact classical description of what such a device
would do on the 2D subspace.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} Receipt bodies and their SHA-256 values are deterministic. Journal entry hashes
include the THESEUS wall-clock timestamp, so they differ between runs.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[{Binet in $\Z[\varphi]$}]\label{prop:binet}
$\varphi^n=F_{n-1}+F_n\varphi$ for $n\ge1$, and $\varphi^n+\psi^n=L_n$, where $\psi=1-\varphi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\varphi^1=0+1\cdot\varphi$. If $\varphi^n=F_{n-1}+F_n\varphi$, then
$\varphi^{n+1}=F_{n-1}\varphi+F_n(\varphi+1)=F_n+F_{n+1}\varphi$. The same induction holds for $\psi$, since
$\psi^2=\psi+1$, and $\varphi+\psi=1$ gives $\varphi^n+\psi^n=2F_{n-1}+F_n=L_n$. With $\sqrt5=2\varphi-1$ this is
$(L_n+F_n\sqrt5)/2$. (Checked in $\Z[\varphi]$ up to $n=364$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$Q^n$, $M_{15}$, Cassini]
$Q^n=\begin{pmatrix}F_{n+1}&F_n\\F_n&F_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}$, because $Q$ acts on $\Z[\varphi]$ as multiplication by
$\varphi$ in the basis $(\varphi,1)$. Hence $\det Q^n=(-1)^n$, $\tr Q^n=L_n$, and
$Q^{15}=\begin{pmatrix}987&610\\610&377\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}219&98\\98&121\end{pmatrix}=M_{15}\pmod{256}$,
with $\det -1$ and trace $1364\equiv84$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Lucas traces of $M_{15}$]
$\tr(M_{15}^n)\equiv L_{15n}\pmod{256}$ for all $n\ge1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$M_{15}\equiv Q^{15}\pmod{256}$, so $M_{15}^n\equiv Q^{15n}$, whose trace is $L_{15n}$. (Checked for $n<64$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Pisano order of $M_8$]
$\operatorname{ord}(M_8)=\pi(256)/\gcd(15,\pi(256))=384/3=128=2^7$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$M_8\equiv Q^{15}\pmod{256}$ and $\operatorname{ord}(Q\bmod256)=\pi(256)=384$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:pisano}). The order
of $g^{15}$ in a cyclic group of order 384 is $384/\gcd(15,384)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:order} gives the same value,
$2^{k-1}$.
\end{proof}
The orbit census of $(\Z/256)^2$ under $M_{15}$ is $\{1{:}4,\ 2{:}6,\ 4{:}12,\ 8{:}24,\ 16{:}48,\ 32{:}96,\
64{:}192,\ 128{:}384\}$ orbits (\code{tests/test\_gl2\_review.py:95}).

\begin{proposition}[Pisot residual]
$|\psi^n|=\varphi^{-n}\le1/F_{n+1}$. So $L_n$ is the nearest integer to $\varphi^n$ for $n\ge2$, and this needs no
rounding operation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\varphi\psi=-1$ gives $|\psi^n|=\varphi^{-n}$. And $\varphi^n=F_{n-1}+F_n\varphi\ge F_{n-1}+F_n=F_{n+1}$, because
$\varphi>1$.
\end{proof}
The audit computes $\psi^n$ exactly in $\Z[\varphi]$ and checks the norm $\varphi^n\psi^n=(-1)^n$ at every scale.

\begin{proposition}[One recurrence, two regimes]\label{prop:dickson}
Let $G$ be the Grover iterate acting on $(\alpha,\beta)$. Then
\[ G=\begin{pmatrix}1-2M/N&2U/N\\-2M/N&2U/N-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \tr G=2c,\quad \det G=1,\quad c=1-2M/N. \]
Hence $\tr G^n=2T_n(c)$. By comparison, $\tr Q^n=L_n$ with $\tr Q=1$ and $\det Q=-1$. Both sequences obey the Dickson
recurrence $x_{n+1}=t\,x_n-d\,x_{n-1}$, with $(t,d)=(2c,1)$ for Grover and $(1,-1)$ for Lucas.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand $\alpha'=2\mu+\alpha$ and $\beta'=2\mu-\beta$. The determinant is $(1-2a)(2u-1)+4au=2(a+u)-1=1$, where
$a=M/N$ and $u=U/N$. Cayley--Hamilton gives $X^{n+1}=tX^n-dX^{n-1}$, so traces obey the same recurrence. With
$x_0=2$ and $x_1=2c$ it is $2T_n(c)$. (Checked exactly for $n<40$ at three $(N,M)$.)
\end{proof}
This is the exact connection between the golden side and the Grover side, and also the exact difference between them.
$Q$ is hyperbolic: its eigenvalues are $\varphi$ and $\psi$, real, with $|\varphi|>1>|\psi|$. That is the Pisot regime,
where powers grow. $G$ is elliptic: its eigenvalues are $e^{\pm2i\theta}$, on the unit circle. That is a rotation,
and the peak comes after $\Theta(\sqrt{N/M})$ turns. The golden structure gives exact, invertible, hash-free
arithmetic for addressing, scheduling and folding. It does not shorten the Grover rotation.

\paragraph{Remark (Lucas traces and closed geodesics; exact part).} This remark comes from
\code{TheoremVerificationSuite.modular\_geodesics\_from\_lucas} in the authors' engine (line 3412), which checks it in
floating point.

$Q$ has $\det-1$, so it lies in $\GL(2,\Z)$ but not in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z)$. Its even powers do lie in
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z)$: $Q^{2k}$ has $\det+1$ and trace $L_{2k}\ge3$, so it is hyperbolic. A hyperbolic element of
trace $t$ translates along its axis in $\mathbb H^2$ by $2\operatorname{arccosh}(t/2)$. Here the identity is exact.
$L_{2k}^2-4=5F_{2k}^2$, so the larger eigenvalue is $(L_{2k}+F_{2k}\sqrt5)/2=\varphi^{2k}$, which is
Proposition~\ref{prop:binet}. Hence
\[ L_{2k}=\varphi^{2k}+\varphi^{-2k},\qquad 2\operatorname{arccosh}(L_{2k}/2)=2\ln\varphi^{2k}=4k\ln\varphi. \]
\code{test\_lucas\_trace\_geodesic\_identity\_exact} checks the integer and $\Z[\varphi]$ parts exactly for
$k\le200$:
\begin{itemize}
\item trace $L_{2k}$ and $\det+1$;
\item $L^2-4=5F^2$;
\item $\varphi^{2k}=(L-F)/2+F\varphi$;
\item $\varphi^{2k}\psi^{2k}=1$.
\end{itemize}
At $k=1$ the trace is 3, the least hyperbolic trace in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z)$, so it gives the shortest closed geodesic
of $\mathbb H^2/\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z)$, of length $4\ln\varphi$. For $k>1$, $Q^{2k}=(Q^2)^k$ traverses that same primitive
geodesic $k$ times. It is not a new primitive geodesic. This is a geometric fact about the golden matrix. It has no
algorithmic consequence here: the Lucas--Pisano schedule (Section~\ref{sec:sched}) uses $L_{3m+1}\bmod2^D$ only as an
odd stride.

\paragraph{Storage (THESEUS).} \code{phyllotaxis\_coordinate} (\code{storage/phyllotaxis.py:52}) assigns record $n$
the golden turn $nF_{64}/F_{66}\bmod1$. This is an exact rational convergent of $1/\varphi^2$, and Cassini gives
$F_{64}F_{66}-F_{65}^2=-1$. Lanes use the Knuth constant $\lfloor2^{64}(\varphi-1)\rfloor=\texttt{0x9E3779B97F4A7C15}$
(:18, :23). It is reproduced from integer square roots by \code{metallic\_conjugate\_64(1)} (:87).
\code{DurableTheseusService} stores and verifies these coordinates at \code{storage/durable.py:165, :216, :291, :409}.

\paragraph{PV scaling.} \code{PVScale(i).field\_factor()} (\code{core/scaling.py:57}) is $\varphi^i$ in
$\mathbb F_{257}[\varphi]$. Since $257\equiv2\pmod5$, the polynomial $x^2-x-1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_{257}$,
so the Frobenius map sends $\varphi$ to $\psi$. Then $\varphi^{258}=\varphi\psi=-1$ and $\varphi^{516}=1$, which is the
``period-516'' reduction at \code{backends/pv\_field.py:19}. This is checked at every scale.

\item \textbf{Exact-certified.} The two integers $N^mT_m(1-2/N)$, for $m=t^*$ and $t^*+1$, have signs $(+,\le0)$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:peak} that is equivalent to $t^*$ being the peak. There is no $\pi$ and no enclosure. This was done
at $10^7$, $10^{10}$ and $10^{12}$; the last took 1.2\,s with $3.1\cdot10^7$-bit integers. From $10^{15}$ the integers
exceed $10^9$ bits, and the attempt at $10^{15}$ was stopped.
\item \textbf{Angle-certified.} $t^*\theta<\arctan(1)\le(t^*+1)\theta$, where $\theta=\arctan(1/\sqrt{N-1})$ is the
Grover angle ($\sin^2\theta=1/N$). Both sides are rational intervals: $\theta$ from integer square roots and the
alternating arctan series, and $\arctan(1)=4\arctan(1/5)-\arctan(1/239)$ (Machin's identity, used as an identity of
arctans). The two strict inequalities are checked in Fractions. No numerical $\pi$ and no floating point are used,
but this is a rigorous rational-interval proof, not a closed form. It certifies the integer $t^*$ at every scale in
at most 14\,ms. It does not compute $P(t^*)$, whose exact denominator has about $(2t^*+1)\log_2N\approx4\cdot10^{40}$
bits at $10^{76}$. The certificate agrees with exact stepping at $N=4,16,100,4096,12345,65536,10^5$ and $10^7$. It
rejects $t^*\pm1$ for $N\le10^5$ and $t^*-1$ at all nine scales (tests).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What scales, and what does not.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{These scale:} the golden identities, the $M_k$ ring facts, the register ($O(Nk)$ bits for $2^N$ states)
and custody, and the certificate for $t^*$. All are polylogarithmic in $S$.
\item \emph{These do not scale:} the stepping ($\Theta(\sqrt N)$ iterations), the exact $P(t^*)$, and above all the
oracle. On classical hardware, learning $M$ still costs $\Theta(W)$ evaluations, so no scale changes the separation of
Section~\ref{sec:sep}.
\item \emph{A curiosity, reported as found:} at $k=\lceil\log_2S\rceil$ we always have $\pi(2^k)=3\cdot2^{k-1}>S$, so
the Pisano reduction $S\bmod\pi(2^k)$ is the identity at these scales. It is a real reduction only for indices beyond
the ring.
\end{itemize}
