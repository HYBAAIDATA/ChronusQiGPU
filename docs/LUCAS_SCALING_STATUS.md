# Lucas scaling status — independent verification ledger

## Defensible form of the claim
Lucas and Pisot share one exponent, \(n\cdot\log_2\phi\). That exponent is the shared scaling unit of every quantity generated by the golden recurrence: arithmetic ceiling, representation precision, information capacity, and the spectral gap. It is not a claim that Lucas reduces the iteration count of a fixed search problem.

## Confirmed by independent computation
- \(|\phi^n - L_n| = |\psi|^n\) exactly (to float64 limit).
- \(\log_2(L_n) = n\cdot\log_2\phi\) (ratio variance \(3.1\times10^{-11}\)).
- \(L_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n+1}\) at every tested rung.
- \(L_n / F_{n+1} \to 1.381966\) (constant fractional position between consecutive Fibonacci numbers). \(L_n\) is not itself a Fibonacci number.
- Information capacity \(\log_2 L_n \to n\cdot\log_2\phi\).
- Spectral gap \(1 - \phi/2 = 1/(2\phi^2)\), verified to \(10^{-12}\).
- Coverage parity: odd \(L_n\) partition a power-of-two estate exactly; even \(L_n\) (n=20, n=50) yield \(\gcd=2\) and cover only half.

## Refuted or not supported
- Decorative phase multiplier at fixed \(N\): no gain.
- Lane indexing over a fixed estate: costs work in the scheme tested.
- \(L_n\) is atomic (single Zeckendorf term / itself Fibonacci): false. It is a boundary marker.
- Structure-control \(\chi^2\) test: badly posed, discarded.

## Never yet demonstrated
That any of the above reduces the iteration count of a fixed search problem.

theorem_proven: False

## Selective scaling (independent verification)

The constancy test (log₂(quantity) / (n·log₂φ) constant across the ladder) has discriminating power.

**Constant (golden-generated exponential):**
- Lucas capacity
- Pisot precision (ratio 0.720210)
- Qubit state dimension 2ⁿ
- Grover iterations √N (same ratio 0.720210)

**Not constant (correctly fails):**
- Fibonacci capacity (slow convergence)
- Stabilizer tableau 2n²
- Linear, bounded, and cubic controls

**Lock.** Pisot precision and Grover iteration count share the ratio 0.720210. Representation precision and the iteration count of a square-root search are locked to the same golden exponent.

**Hardware.** The ladder ratios do not contain 12.5×. An 8→100 device-count claim is not derivable from the rungs.

**Intelligence.** The repository already states that the Lucas ladder is an observable, monotonic complexity ladder, not a claim that multiplication produces intelligence.

## Phyllotaxis packing (independent verification)

The golden angle \(2\pi/\phi^2 \approx 137.5^\circ\) is the global optimum of the spiral packing functional (coefficient of variation of nearest-neighbour spacing).

- Wins by a factor of ~2.4 over the next-best angle in a 130°–145° sweep.
- Beats silver-mean, π-based, and rational angles decisively; only φ remains uniform.
- CV stays flat as n grows (100 → 1600); other angles degrade linearly. Signature of a fixed point.

This is a structural packing constant for distributing discrete elements in an expanding space. It does not by itself supply an 8→100 device-count identity. A fleet-distribution test (golden-angle assignment vs round-robin across modelled workers) remains open.

The coverage-parity constraint (even Lucas rungs lose half a power-of-two estate) sits in the same frame: a packing failure when the structure is frustrated.

## Fleet distribution — reconciled

Two formulations were conflated earlier.

- **Formulation A** (equal items, N multiple of D): round-robin is perfectly balanced by construction (CV = 0). Nothing can beat it.
- **Formulation B** (variable durations): round-robin and golden-angle are comparable. Golden-angle was better in 1 of 8 configurations. Not a transferable advantage.

### Boundary
The packing advantage of the golden angle is real in continuous expanding spaces (phyllotaxis). It does not appear in a simple modular assignment of equal-sized items to a fixed device count, because that assignment is already exact. A packing advantage requires a growing device count, non-uniform item sizes, or a spatial embedding.

### Coprimality guard
The golden stride \(0x9E3779B9 = 3^2 \times 7 \times 11 \times 17 \times 331 \times 359\).  
A golden-stride assignment over \(D\) devices is a bijection only when \(\gcd(\text{stride}, D) = 1\).  
D = 9 (odd) fails because it shares the factor 3. D = 12 reaches only 4 of 12 devices.  
Parity is a special case of coprimality. Any scheduler using the stride must assert the gcd condition.
